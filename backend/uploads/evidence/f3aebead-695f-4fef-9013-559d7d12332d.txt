Information Security Management Framework
Version 2.0 | Effective Date: January 1, 2026 | Classification: Internal

Approved by: Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
Review Cycle: Annual | Next Review: January 1, 2027

SECTION 1 — ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT AND GOVERNANCE (GV)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1.1 Organizational Mission and Cybersecurity Context

NextStep IT provides managed IT services, cloud infrastructure, and consulting
solutions to enterprise clients. Cybersecurity is a strategic business priority.
The organization operates in alignment with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0
(NIST CSF 2.0) as its primary security governance framework.

All cybersecurity activities are conducted in support of the organization's
mission to deliver reliable, secure, and compliant technology services. The
cybersecurity program considers regulatory obligations (GDPR, ISO 27001),
contractual requirements with clients, and the threat landscape relevant to
managed service providers.

Stakeholder expectations, legal obligations, and business objectives are
formally reviewed annually and documented in the Cybersecurity Strategy
document maintained by the CISO office.

1.2 Cybersecurity Risk Management Strategy

The organization adopts a risk-based approach to cybersecurity. Risk tolerance
and risk appetite are formally defined by the Board of Directors and CISO,
documented in the Enterprise Risk Appetite Statement, and reviewed annually.

Risk management objectives:
- Protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability of all information assets
- Minimize likelihood and impact of cybersecurity incidents
- Comply with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements
- Maintain client trust and operational continuity

Risk management decisions are prioritized based on asset criticality, threat
likelihood, potential business impact, and cost-effectiveness of controls.
All residual risks exceeding defined tolerance thresholds require formal
acceptance by senior management.

The organization participates in cybersecurity information sharing communities
and threat intelligence networks to improve situational awareness.

1.3 Roles, Responsibilities, and Accountability

Board of Directors: Approves cybersecurity strategy and risk appetite; receives
quarterly cybersecurity reports.

Chief Executive Officer (CEO): Accountable for organizational cybersecurity
posture; ensures adequate resources are allocated.

Chief Information Security Officer (CISO): Owns the cybersecurity program;
responsible for policy development, risk management, incident response, and
compliance.

Chief Technology Officer (CTO): Responsible for secure architecture and
engineering practices; ensures security-by-design in all systems.

IT Security Team: Implements technical controls; monitors systems; responds
to security events; conducts vulnerability assessments.

Department Managers: Accountable for cybersecurity compliance within their
departments; ensure staff complete mandatory security training.

All Employees: Responsible for following security policies; reporting suspicious
activity; protecting company and client data.

Third-Party Suppliers: Required to meet cybersecurity standards defined in
supplier agreements; subject to periodic security assessments.

A RACI matrix defining cybersecurity responsibilities is maintained and
reviewed annually by Human Resources and the CISO.

1.4 Cybersecurity Policies and Procedures

This document constitutes the master cybersecurity policy. Supporting policies
include:

- Acceptable Use Policy
- Access Control Policy
- Data Classification and Handling Policy
- Incident Response Policy and Procedures
- Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy
- Vulnerability Management Policy
- Third-Party Risk Management Policy
- Secure Development Lifecycle Policy
- Password and Authentication Policy
- Physical Security Policy
- Data Retention and Destruction Policy
- Remote Work and Mobile Device Policy

All policies are:
- Approved by the CISO and relevant executives
- Communicated to all relevant personnel upon hire and annually
- Reviewed and updated at minimum annually or after significant incidents
- Version-controlled and accessible via the internal policy portal
- Aligned with NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022

Non-compliance with policies is subject to disciplinary action, up to and
including termination of employment or contract.

1.5 Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management

All third-party vendors, suppliers, and service providers with access to
organizational systems or data are subject to:

- Pre-engagement security assessment (questionnaire + risk scoring)
- Contractual cybersecurity requirements (data protection, breach notification,
  right to audit, minimum security standards)
- Ongoing monitoring throughout the relationship
- Annual reassessment for high-risk and critical suppliers
- Formal offboarding procedures including access revocation and data return

Supplier cybersecurity requirements are included in all contracts and
procurement processes. Critical suppliers are reviewed quarterly.

SECTION 2 — IDENTIFY (ID)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2.1 Asset Management

The organization maintains a comprehensive inventory of all information assets
including:

Hardware Assets: Servers, workstations, laptops, network devices, mobile
devices, storage media, printers, and IoT devices.

Software Assets: Licensed applications, open-source software, operating
systems, cloud services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), and internal applications.

Data Assets: Classified by sensitivity (Public, Internal, Confidential,
Restricted) with defined handling, storage, and disposal requirements.

Network Assets: Network segments, IP address ranges, VPNs, firewalls,
wireless access points, and external-facing services.

The asset inventory is maintained in the Configuration Management Database
(CMDB) and updated:
- Automatically via network discovery tools (weekly scans)
- Manually upon procurement or decommissioning of assets
- During quarterly reconciliation reviews

Each asset record includes: owner, classification, location, lifecycle status,
and associated security controls. Assets are assigned to accountable owners
who are responsible for ensuring appropriate protection.

Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) is maintained for all internally developed
and third-party applications.

2.2 Risk Assessment

Cybersecurity risk assessments are conducted:
- Annually for the full organization
- Before major system changes, new projects, or technology adoptions
- After significant security incidents
- When the threat landscape changes materially

Risk assessment methodology:
1. Asset identification and valuation
2. Threat identification (using threat intelligence and MITRE ATT&CK framework)
3. Vulnerability identification (via scanning and assessment tools)
4. Likelihood and impact analysis
5. Risk scoring and prioritization
6. Control selection and treatment planning
7. Residual risk evaluation
8. Risk acceptance or escalation

Risk register is maintained by the CISO and reviewed monthly by the security
team and quarterly by senior management.

Threat intelligence from government agencies (CISA, ENISA), industry ISACs,
and commercial feeds is continuously monitored and incorporated into risk
assessments.

2.3 Improvement and Lessons Learned

The organization continuously improves its cybersecurity posture through:

- Post-incident reviews and root cause analysis after all significant incidents
- Annual cybersecurity maturity assessments against NIST CSF 2.0
- Internal and external security audits and penetration tests
- Tracking of key risk indicators (KRIs) and key performance indicators (KPIs)
- Benchmarking against industry peers and best practices
- Integration of lessons learned from industry incidents and threat intelligence

Improvement actions are tracked in the risk register with assigned owners and
target completion dates. Progress is reported monthly to the CISO and quarterly
to the Board.

SECTION 3 — PROTECT (PR)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

3.1 Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control

Access to all systems and data is granted on a least-privilege, need-to-know
basis and is formally approved by the data/system owner.

User Identity and Access Management:
- All users are assigned unique, non-shared accounts
- Account creation requires formal approval from the hiring manager and IT
- Privileged accounts (admin, root, service accounts) are strictly controlled,
  inventoried separately, and require additional justification
- Accounts are disabled within 24 hours of employee termination
- Quarterly access reviews are conducted; unused accounts are disabled after
  90 days of inactivity
- Temporary access is time-limited and documented

Authentication Requirements:
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is mandatory for all remote access, cloud
  services, administrative interfaces, and email
- Passwords must meet minimum complexity requirements:
  Minimum 12 characters, mix of upper/lower case, numbers, and symbols
- Passwords are never shared or reused across systems
- Password managers are provided and recommended for all staff
- Privileged accounts use hardware security keys where technically feasible
- Single Sign-On (SSO) is implemented across all major corporate applications
- Service accounts use certificate-based authentication where possible

Session Management:
- Inactive sessions time out after 15 minutes for privileged systems and
  30 minutes for standard systems
- Remote desktop and VPN sessions log off after 4 hours of inactivity
- Concurrent sessions are limited based on role requirements

Physical and Logical Separation:
- Production, staging, and development environments are strictly separated
- Administrative networks are isolated from user networks
- Guest network access is restricted from corporate resources

3.2 Awareness and Training

All personnel receive cybersecurity training appropriate to their role:

General Security Awareness (All Staff):
- Mandatory training completed within 30 days of hire
- Annual refresher training (minimum 2 hours)
- Topics: phishing recognition, password hygiene, data handling, incident
  reporting, social engineering, remote work security, mobile device security
- Training completion is tracked; non-completion escalated to managers

Specialized Training (IT and Security Staff):
- Role-specific training aligned to job responsibilities
- Annual certifications maintained (CISSP, CISM, CEH, CompTIA Security+)
- Participation in tabletop exercises and incident response drills

Developer Secure Coding Training:
- Mandatory for all software developers
- Covers OWASP Top 10, secure coding practices, dependency management
- Completed before access to production code repositories

Phishing Simulations:
- Monthly simulated phishing campaigns for all staff
- Immediate training intervention for users who fail simulations
- Trends reported quarterly to management

Social Engineering Awareness:
- Training covers vishing, pretexting, and impersonation attacks
- Clear verification procedures for sensitive requests (financial transactions,
  access changes) via out-of-band channels

3.3 Data Security

Data Classification:
- Public: No restrictions on distribution
- Internal: For internal use only, not for external sharing without authorization
- Confidential: Restricted to authorized personnel; encryption required
- Restricted: Highest sensitivity; strict access controls, audit logging,
  MFA required for access

Data Protection Controls:
- Encryption at rest: AES-256 for all Confidential and Restricted data
- Encryption in transit: TLS 1.2 minimum (TLS 1.3 preferred) for all data
  transmissions; unencrypted protocols (HTTP, FTP, Telnet) prohibited
- Database encryption enabled for all production databases containing
  personal or sensitive data
- Full disk encryption on all laptops and mobile devices (BitLocker/FileVault)
- USB and removable media use requires authorization and encryption
- Personal data processed in compliance with GDPR and applicable privacy laws
- Data residency requirements documented and enforced for each data category

Data Handling and Disposal:
- Clear desk policy enforced for paper documents
- Secure printing used for sensitive documents
- Paper documents shredded using cross-cut shredders
- Digital media sanitized using DoD 5220.22-M standard before disposal
- Data retention periods defined per data category and regulatory requirements
- Data destruction certificates maintained for Confidential and Restricted data

Data Loss Prevention (DLP):
- DLP tools monitor and control data egress via email, web, USB, and cloud
- Rules tuned to detect and prevent unauthorized transmission of sensitive data
- DLP alerts reviewed daily by the security team

3.4 Platform Security

Endpoint Security:
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) deployed on all managed endpoints
- Anti-malware with real-time protection, updated at least daily
- Host-based firewalls enabled on all workstations and servers
- Unauthorized software installation prohibited; application whitelisting on
  high-risk systems
- Patch management: Critical patches applied within 72 hours; high within 7
  days; medium within 30 days; low within 90 days
- Automated patch deployment for operating systems and common applications
- Legacy operating systems not receiving security updates prohibited in
  production environments

Network Security:
- Network segmentation with firewall rules between segments
- Default-deny firewall policy; all permitted traffic explicitly authorized
- Intrusion Detection and Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS) on network perimeter
  and between critical segments
- DNS filtering to block malicious domains
- Email security gateway with anti-phishing, anti-spam, and sandboxing
- Web proxy with SSL inspection for outbound web traffic
- All remote access via VPN with MFA; split tunneling restricted
- Wi-Fi networks segmented; guest Wi-Fi isolated from corporate network

Cloud Security:
- Cloud services approved via formal procurement and security review process
- Cloud Security Posture Management (CSPM) tools monitor cloud configuration
- Principle of least privilege applied to all cloud IAM roles
- Multi-account architecture with separate production, development, and
  management accounts
- Cloud resource tagging policy enforced; untagged resources flagged
- Cloud access logs retained for minimum 12 months

Secure Configuration:
- Hardening baselines applied to all systems (CIS Benchmarks)
- Default accounts and passwords changed before deployment
- Unnecessary services, ports, and protocols disabled
- Configuration compliance scanned monthly; deviations remediated promptly
- Infrastructure as Code (IaC) templates reviewed for security before deployment
- Secrets (API keys, passwords, certificates) never hardcoded; stored in
  approved secrets management systems

3.5 Infrastructure Resilience

Business Continuity Controls:
- Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO) defined
  for all critical systems
- Redundant infrastructure for all Tier 1 systems (N+1 minimum)
- Geographically distributed data centers for disaster recovery
- Data backups performed daily (incremental) and weekly (full)
- Backups stored offline or in immutable storage to protect against ransomware
- Backup restoration tested quarterly; results documented
- Failover tested annually for all critical systems

Anti-Ransomware Controls:
- Immutable backups in isolated environments not accessible from production
- Network segmentation to limit lateral movement
- EDR with ransomware behavioral detection
- Privileged access management to protect backup systems

Capacity Management:
- Infrastructure capacity monitored continuously with automated alerting
- Capacity planning conducted quarterly; growth projections maintained 12 months ahead

SECTION 4 — DETECT (DE)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

4.1 Continuous Monitoring

The organization operates a Security Operations Center (SOC) with 24/7
monitoring capabilities.

Security Information and Event Management (SIEM):
- All security-relevant logs ingested into SIEM (centralized logging)
- Log sources: firewalls, IDS/IPS, endpoints, servers, cloud services,
  authentication systems, network devices, applications
- Logs retained for minimum 12 months online; 7 years in archive
- Log integrity protected; log deletion requires dual authorization
- Alerts tuned to minimize false positives while maintaining detection fidelity

Monitoring Coverage:
- Network traffic analysis for anomalous patterns
- User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA) to detect insider threats
- Privileged account activity monitoring (all administrative actions logged)
- Data access monitoring for sensitive data stores
- Cloud activity logs (CloudTrail, Azure Monitor, GCP Audit Logs)
- Authentication events: failed logins, privilege escalation, off-hours access
- Email security events: phishing attempts, malicious attachments
- Endpoint telemetry: process execution, file changes, network connections
- Vulnerability scanner results integrated into SIEM

Threat Intelligence Integration:
- Commercial and open-source threat intelligence feeds integrated into SIEM
- Indicators of Compromise (IoCs) automatically checked against all log sources
- Threat intelligence used to tune detection rules and prioritize responses
- Intelligence shared with trusted partner organizations via ISAC membership

4.2 Adverse Event Analysis

Security events are triaged, correlated, and analyzed by the SOC team:

Alert Handling Process:
1. Alert generated by detection tools (SIEM, EDR, IDS/IPS)
2. Level 1 SOC analyst reviews and performs initial triage within 15 minutes
3. False positives documented and rules updated
4. True positives escalated per severity classification:
   - Critical: Immediate escalation to security incident response team
   - High: Response within 1 hour
   - Medium: Response within 4 hours
   - Low: Response within 24 hours

Event Correlation:
- SIEM correlation rules detect multi-stage attack patterns
- Alerts from multiple sources correlated to identify incidents
- Historical analysis used to detect slow-moving threats (APTs)
- Machine learning anomaly detection supplements rule-based detection

Forensic Capabilities:
- Endpoint forensic tools available for memory and disk analysis
- Network packet capture capability on key segments
- Forensic investigation procedures documented and tested annually
- Chain of custody procedures maintained for all evidence

SECTION 5 — RESPOND (RS)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

5.1 Incident Management

The organization maintains a formal Incident Response Plan (IRP) tested
annually via tabletop exercises and periodic live drills.

Incident Classification:
- P1 Critical: Active breach, ransomware, data exfiltration in progress —
  immediate response; executive notification within 30 minutes
- P2 High: Confirmed malware, unauthorized access to sensitive systems —
  response within 1 hour; management notification within 2 hours
- P3 Medium: Suspicious activity, policy violations, potential compromise —
  response within 4 hours
- P4 Low: Isolated events, minor policy violations — response within 24 hours

Incident Response Team (IRT):
- Incident Commander (CISO or designated)
- Technical Lead (senior security engineer)
- Communications Lead (handles internal and external communications)
- Legal Counsel (engaged for P1/P2 incidents)
- HR Representative (for insider threat incidents)
- External Incident Response Retainer (engaged for major incidents)

5.2 Incident Analysis

All security incidents undergo structured analysis:

Initial Analysis:
- Confirm incident versus false positive
- Identify affected systems, data, and users
- Determine attack vector and initial compromise point
- Assess potential scope and impact

Deep Analysis:
- Timeline reconstruction of attacker activity
- Identification of all compromised systems and accounts
- Determination of data accessed, exfiltrated, or modified
- Identification of persistence mechanisms

Evidence Preservation:
- System images and memory dumps captured before remediation where feasible
- All investigative findings documented with timestamps
- Chain of custody maintained for potential legal proceedings

5.3 Incident Mitigation and Containment

Containment Strategies:
- Network isolation of compromised systems
- Account lockout and password resets for compromised credentials
- Firewall rule changes to block attacker IP addresses
- Revocation of compromised API keys and certificates
- Application of emergency patches for exploited vulnerabilities

Eradication:
- Removal of malware, backdoors, and unauthorized tools
- Reimaging of compromised systems where required
- Removal of attacker persistence mechanisms (scheduled tasks, registry entries)
- Verification that eradication is complete before recovery

Recovery:
- Systems restored from clean backups
- Security controls verified before returning systems to production
- Enhanced monitoring applied post-recovery
- Lessons learned documented and improvement actions initiated

5.4 Communication and Reporting

Internal Communications:
- Executive team notified per incident severity classification
- All-staff communications for incidents affecting employee data or operations
- Regular status updates during active incidents (every 2 hours for P1/P2)

External Communications:
- Regulatory notifications: Data breaches reported to supervisory authorities
  within 72 hours (GDPR Article 33) where required
- Affected individuals notified without undue delay for high-risk breaches
- Client notifications per contractual obligations
- Law enforcement contacted for criminal activity
- Coordinated public disclosure for significant incidents

Communication principles:
- All external communications approved by Legal and CISO before release
- No technical details disclosed that could assist attackers
- Factual, accurate, and timely information provided to stakeholders

SECTION 6 — RECOVER (RC)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

6.1 Recovery Planning and Execution

Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Plans (BCP/DRP) are:
- Documented for all critical business processes and IT systems
- Reviewed and updated annually or after significant changes
- Tested via tabletop exercises (twice annually) and full failover tests (annually)
- Aligned with business RTO and RPO requirements

Recovery Priorities (per Business Impact Analysis):
Tier 1 (RTO < 4 hours, RPO < 1 hour):
  - Customer-facing services and platforms
  - Authentication and identity systems
  - Security monitoring and SOC tools
Tier 2 (RTO < 8 hours, RPO < 4 hours):
  - Internal collaboration tools (email, video conferencing)
  - Financial systems
  - Client management platforms
Tier 3 (RTO < 24 hours, RPO < 8 hours):
  - Reporting and analytics systems
  - Development environments
  - Internal tools and utilities

Recovery Procedures:
- Step-by-step recovery runbooks maintained for all Tier 1 systems
- Runbooks stored offline and in geographically separate locations
- Recovery team roles and contact information maintained and tested
- Vendor support contacts and service level agreements documented

6.2 Communication During Recovery

During recovery from significant incidents:
- Recovery status communicated to executive team every 2 hours
- Client communication managed by account managers with CISO oversight
- Regular updates posted to internal status page
- External status page updated for client-facing service disruptions
- Media inquiries handled exclusively by Communications and Legal

Post-recovery communications:
- Post-incident report issued within 5 business days
- Root cause and remediation actions communicated to stakeholders
- Regulatory reports submitted within required timeframes
- Client reports provided per contractual requirements

6.3 Post-Incident Improvement

After every P1 and P2 incident, and significant P3 incidents:
- Formal post-incident review (PIR) conducted within 10 business days
- Root cause analysis completed using structured methodology (5 Whys, Ishikawa)
- Improvement actions identified, assigned, and tracked to completion
- Lessons learned distributed to relevant teams
- Policy and procedure updates implemented within 30 days
- Detection and response capabilities enhanced based on gaps identified

Annual cybersecurity improvement program:
- Maturity assessment against NIST CSF 2.0 (target: Tier 3 or higher)
- Penetration testing by external third party
- Red team exercises to test detection and response capabilities
- Security architecture review
- Training and awareness program refresh

SECTION 7 — COMPLIANCE AND AUDIT
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

7.1 Compliance Monitoring

The organization monitors compliance with this policy and supporting
standards through:
- Automated compliance scanning (daily for critical controls)
- Monthly internal security reviews
- Quarterly management reviews of key security metrics
- Annual external audit by accredited third-party auditors

Non-compliance findings are tracked in the risk register with remediation
plans and target dates.

7.2 Audit Rights

The organization reserves the right to:
- Monitor all activity on company-owned systems and networks
- Conduct security assessments of third-party vendors
- Request evidence of compliance from all staff and contractors
- Inspect any company-owned device at any time

Users of company systems have no expectation of privacy in their use of
company resources, consistent with applicable law.

7.3 Policy Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy may be granted only by the CISO and require:
- Written request with business justification
- Risk assessment of the exception
- Compensating controls to reduce risk
- Time-limited approval with expiration date
- Annual review and renewal if continued exception is required

All approved exceptions are documented in the exception register maintained
by the CISO.
